          What is SpendStory?

          In plain English

          A bank statement is a wall of cryptic text — UPI-SWIGGY-swiggy@ybl tells you almost nothing at a glance. SpendStory reads that wall for you and turns it into a picture: how much went out, how much came in, what you spent it on, and which charges look unusual.

          You upload your statement PDF, wait a few seconds, and get a dashboard. No account to create, no forms to fill in, nothing to install.

          Step by step

          1. Upload. Drop in your statement PDF. Locked with a password? You'll be asked for it — it's used to open the file and then discarded.
          2. We read it. Your bank is auto-detected, and every transaction is pulled out with its date, description and amount.
          3. We sort it. Each transaction is matched to a category — food, transport, bills, shopping and so on — from the merchant name in the description.
          4. You explore. Charts, top merchants, recurring subscriptions, and unusually large payments. Tap any category to filter, or search for a specific merchant.
          5. Optionally, you export. A paid one-off download gives you the same data as an Excel report, a Tally XML import, or an accounting CSV.

          What happens to your data

          • Your PDF is processed in memory and never written to disk on our side.
          • Nothing about your statement is stored on our servers after the response is sent — there is no database of your transactions.
          • "Past analyses" lives in your browser's own storage, on this device only. It keeps a small summary, never the original PDF. Clear it any time from that panel — worth doing on a shared computer.
          • Payments go through Razorpay's own hosted checkout. Your card or UPI details are entered on their page, not ours, and we never receive them.

          For the technically minded

          • Text is extracted from the PDF and parsed with per-bank layout rules — currently HDFC, SBI, Axis, PNB, Indian Overseas and City Union, auto-detected from the document itself.
          • Categorisation is a deterministic keyword match against a merchant dictionary, not a language model. The same statement always produces the same categories, and nothing is sent to any AI provider.
          • "Unusually large" is a statistical outlier check against your own spending distribution, not a fixed rupee threshold — so it adapts to how much you normally spend.
          • Recurring subscriptions are detected by finding near-identical amounts to the same merchant at roughly monthly intervals.
          • Drop in several statements at once and they're merged into a single timeline, deduplicated where the date ranges overlap.

          Where it can be wrong

          • A statement from an unsupported bank, or an unusual layout from a supported one, may extract partially or not at all. Nothing is silently guessed — you'll see it in the numbers.
          • A merchant that isn't in the keyword dictionary lands in a general category rather than being invented into a specific one.
          • Scanned or image-only PDFs have no extractable text and won't work; you need the digital statement your bank issues.
